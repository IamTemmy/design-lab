01 / SUBJECT

What the system found

I'm a hardware-first engineer finishing a master's in electrical engineering at Jackson State. My work sits where the sensor meets the water, the battery meets the motor, and the model meets a machine that actually has to run it.

Most of what I build starts as a question about whether something will hold up: will this enclosure survive a tidal cycle, will this pack deliver current under load, will this network still infer fast enough once it's off the workstation. The answers come from instrumenting things and watching them fail, not from the datasheet.

I'm aiming at hardware and test engineering roles in autonomous vehicles — the part of the stack where the perception system stops being a diagram and becomes something bolted to a moving object.

02 / OBSERVATIONS

Selected work

Oyster Gaping Monitor

Bio-integrated aquatic sensing · field deployed

A sensor that rides on a living animal and reports how often it opens its shell — a direct read on water quality and stress. The hard part was never the measurement. It was building something that stays sealed, stays calibrated and keeps logging while it sits submerged in a tidal environment that wants to corrode, foul and flood it.

End-to-End Steering Net

PilotNet reproduction · edge inference

A reproduction of the classic camera-to-steering-angle convolutional network, rebuilt to understand what the architecture is actually doing rather than to chase a benchmark. The interesting work started after training: what happens to latency and accuracy when the same model has to run on hardware that fits in a vehicle instead of under a desk.

Hoverboard Robot Platform

Salvaged drivetrain · student build program

Stripped hoverboards turned into a repeatable robot base: reuse the pack and its battery management, drive the hub motors with external BLDC controllers, put an ESP32 on top for control. Designed so several student teams could each build one without every group re-solving the same power and motor problems from scratch.

Release-to-Incident Intelligence

Evidence-driven triage · web tooling

When something breaks in production, the first question is always which change caused it. This is a dashboard that ties incidents back to the releases that preceded them and shows the evidence for the link, so triage becomes a matter of reading the record rather than trusting whoever remembers the most.

ros2_control_demos

Upstream contribution · robotics middleware

A fix contributed back to the ROS 2 control demos, in the frame-prefix handling that trips people up when they run more than one robot in the same namespace. Small change, but the kind that only surfaces once you've actually tried to bring up the stack yourself.
